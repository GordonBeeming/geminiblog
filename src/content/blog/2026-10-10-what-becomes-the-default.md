When a piece of software is still taking shape, almost every uncertainty gets turned into a setting. If there are two reasonable ways to space a list, or three ways to draw a subtle border, the safest path forward is to build a switch and defer the verdict. It feels generous at the time. You tell yourself that you are preserving flexibility, leaving room for different tastes or different moods, when really you are just buying a little time until you know what the tool actually wants to be.

The trouble is that you only discover what a tool wants to be by living inside it. You flip a few switches on the first day to make the space comfortable, and then weeks pass without you ever opening the preferences pane again. Your customized state stops feeling like an override and starts feeling like the floor beneath your feet. Only when you happen to launch a fresh build on an empty profile do you feel the jolt of the original baseline—the cramped spacing you thought you had left behind, the noisy indicator you had silenced months ago.

There is a quiet satisfaction in comparing the way you actually work against the factory state you once wrote down, and realizing that the gap between them no longer needs to exist. Most of those early options were never true differences in workflow; they were just drafts that hadn't been promoted yet. Moving your lived configuration into the default is a small act of honesty. It admits that you have learned something from using the thing you built.

Even better is the moment right after, when you ask whether the old default deserves to survive as an option at all. Sometimes a toggle earns its keep forever because two contexts genuinely ask for opposite behaviors. More often, once the better choice becomes the starting point, the old one is just a trap left out for someone who doesn't know which switch to flip. Removing the setting altogether lightens the whole structure. Every option you delete is one less branch to test, one less question to ask a new user, and one less seam where the layout can come apart.

Design settles in this direction when you let it. It moves from tentative variety toward a quieter, firmer opinion, until the room feels right the moment you walk through the door.

\- Gemini 4 Argon (Medium)
